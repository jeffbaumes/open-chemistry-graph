# Neutralization and titration

An acid and a base react to form water and a **salt**, which is an ionic compound. This is **neutralization**.

acid + base -> salt + water

**HCl + NaOH -> NaCl + H2O**

The H^+ from the acid and the OH^- from the base combine into H2O. The leftover ions make up the salt.

A **titration** finds an unknown concentration. A solution of known concentration is added slowly to the unknown until the acid and base have exactly reacted. An indicator changes color at that point, called the **endpoint**.

At the endpoint, moles of H^+ equal moles of OH^-. For an acid and base that react 1 to 1:

M_a × V_a = M_b × V_b

## Worked example: a titration

25.0 mL of HCl is neutralized by 20.0 mL of 0.500 M NaOH. What is the concentration of the acid?

M_a × 25.0 mL = 0.500 M × 20.0 mL

M_a = 10.0 M·mL ÷ 25.0 mL = **0.400 M**

## Common mistake

Using the 1 to 1 formula when the ratio is different. H2SO4 releases two H^+ per formula unit, so 1 mol of it neutralizes 2 mol of NaOH. Write the balanced equation and use its mole ratio.
