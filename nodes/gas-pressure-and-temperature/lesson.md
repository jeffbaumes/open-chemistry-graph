# Pressure and temperature

Gas particles move fast in straight lines and collide with each other and with the walls of their container. This picture is the **kinetic molecular theory**.

**Pressure** is the force of those collisions per unit of area. More collisions, or harder ones, mean higher pressure.

1 atm = 760 mmHg = 101.3 kPa

**Temperature** measures the average kinetic energy of the particles. Gas calculations need the **Kelvin** scale, which starts at absolute zero, the lowest possible temperature.

K = °C + 273

Standard temperature and pressure (**STP**) is 273 K and 1 atm.

## Worked example: convert 25 °C to kelvins

25 + 273 = **298 K**

## Worked example: convert 1.25 atm to mmHg

1.25 atm × (760 mmHg / 1 atm) = **950 mmHg**

## Common mistake

Using Celsius in gas laws. A gas at 20 °C is not twice as hot as one at 10 °C. In kelvins those are 293 K and 283 K, a difference of only a few percent.
