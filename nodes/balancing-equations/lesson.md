# Balancing chemical equations

Atoms are not created or destroyed in a reaction, so each element must have the same number of atoms on both sides of the arrow.

You balance by changing **coefficients** (the numbers in front), never **subscripts**. Changing a subscript changes the substance: H2O is water, H2O2 is hydrogen peroxide.

## Worked example: H2 + O2 -> H2O

Count atoms as written:

| Element | Left | Right |
|---|---|---|
| H | 2 | 2 |
| O | 2 | 1 |

Oxygen is short on the right, so put a 2 in front of H2O. That makes 4 H on the right, so put a 2 in front of H2.

**2 H2 + O2 -> 2 H2O**

| Element | Left | Right |
|---|---|---|
| H | 4 | 4 |
| O | 2 | 2 |

## Tips

- Balance elements that appear in only one compound on each side first.
- Leave elements that appear alone (like O2 or Fe) for last, since changing them affects nothing else.
- Finish by reducing to the smallest whole numbers: 4 H2 + 2 O2 -> 4 H2O is balanced but not in lowest terms.
