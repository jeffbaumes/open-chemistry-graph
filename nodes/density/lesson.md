# Density

**Density** is the mass of a substance per unit of volume.

density = mass ÷ volume, or d = m / V

Common units are g/mL for liquids and g/cm^3 for solids. These are the same size, because 1 mL = 1 cm^3.

Density is a property of the substance, not the sample. A gold ring and a gold bar have the same density, 19.3 g/cm^3. That makes density useful for identifying materials.

An object floats in a liquid if it is less dense than the liquid. Water has a density of 1.00 g/mL.

## Worked example: find a density

A metal block has a mass of 54.0 g and a volume of 20.0 cm^3.

d = 54.0 g ÷ 20.0 cm^3 = **2.70 g/cm^3**

That matches aluminum.

## Worked example: find a mass

What is the mass of 15.0 mL of ethanol, density 0.789 g/mL? Rearrange to m = d × V.

m = 0.789 g/mL × 15.0 mL = **11.8 g**

## Common mistake

Thinking heavy objects sink and light ones float. A steel ship floats and a small pebble sinks. What matters is mass compared with volume, not mass alone.
