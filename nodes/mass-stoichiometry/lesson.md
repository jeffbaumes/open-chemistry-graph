# Mass-to-mass stoichiometry

Balances measure grams, but equations work in moles. So a mass-to-mass problem always takes three steps.

1. **Grams to moles** of the given substance, using its molar mass.
2. **Moles to moles**, using the mole ratio from the balanced equation.
3. **Moles to grams** of the wanted substance, using its molar mass.

**grams A -> moles A -> moles B -> grams B**

## Worked example: what mass of water forms when 8.00 g of H2 burns?

2 H2 + O2 -> 2 H2O

Step 1: 8.00 g H2 ÷ 2.016 g/mol = 3.97 mol H2

Step 2: 3.97 mol H2 × (2 mol H2O / 2 mol H2) = 3.97 mol H2O

Step 3: 3.97 mol H2O × 18.015 g/mol = **71.5 g H2O**

## Common mistake

Using a mass ratio in place of a mole ratio. The coefficients compare moles, never grams. Going straight from grams of one substance to grams of another skips the step where the equation is actually used.
