# Charles's law

At constant pressure, the volume of a fixed amount of gas is **directly proportional** to its temperature in kelvins.

V_1 / T_1 = V_2 / T_2

Heating a gas makes its particles move faster and hit the walls harder. If the container can expand, it does, until the pressure inside matches the pressure outside again.

## Worked example: warming a balloon

A balloon has a volume of 2.00 L at 300 K. What is its volume at 450 K, at the same pressure?

V_1 / T_1 = V_2 / T_2

2.00 L / 300 K = V_2 / 450 K

V_2 = 2.00 L × 450 K ÷ 300 K = **3.00 L**

Check: the temperature went up, so the volume should go up. It did.

When pressure, volume and temperature all change, use the **combined gas law**:

P_1 × V_1 / T_1 = P_2 × V_2 / T_2

## Common mistake

Using degrees Celsius. Going from 20 °C to 40 °C does not double the volume. In kelvins that is 293 K to 313 K, an increase of about 7%.
