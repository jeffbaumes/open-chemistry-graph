# Empirical formulas

The **empirical formula** is the simplest whole-number ratio of atoms in a compound. The **molecular formula** is the actual number of atoms in a molecule. Glucose has the molecular formula C6H12O6 and the empirical formula CH2O.

To find an empirical formula:

1. Start with the mass of each element. If given percentages, assume a 100 g sample so each percent becomes grams.
2. Convert each mass to moles.
3. Divide every mole value by the smallest one.
4. If a result is not close to a whole number, multiply all of them by a small number (2, 3 and so on) until they are.

## Worked example: 40.0% C, 6.7% H, 53.3% O

In 100 g there are 40.0 g of C, 6.7 g of H and 53.3 g of O.

| Element | Moles | Divided by smallest |
|---|---|---|
| C | 40.0 ÷ 12.011 = 3.33 | 1.00 |
| H | 6.7 ÷ 1.008 = 6.65 | 2.00 |
| O | 53.3 ÷ 15.999 = 3.33 | 1.00 |

Empirical formula: **CH2O**

To get a molecular formula, divide the compound's molar mass by the empirical formula mass, then multiply every subscript by the result. If CH2O (30.03 g/mol) has a molar mass of 180.16 g/mol, the multiplier is 6 and the molecular formula is C6H12O6.

## Common mistake

Rounding too early. A ratio of 1.5 is not 2. Multiply everything by 2 to get whole numbers: 1 to 1.5 becomes 2 to 3.
