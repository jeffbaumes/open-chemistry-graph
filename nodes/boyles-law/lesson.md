# Boyle's law

At constant temperature, the pressure and volume of a fixed amount of gas are **inversely proportional**. When one goes up, the other goes down by the same factor.

P_1 × V_1 = P_2 × V_2

Squeezing a gas into a smaller space makes its particles hit the walls more often, so the pressure rises. Halve the volume and the pressure doubles.

## Worked example: compressing a gas

A gas occupies 4.00 L at 1.00 atm. What is its volume at 2.50 atm, at the same temperature?

P_1 × V_1 = P_2 × V_2

1.00 atm × 4.00 L = 2.50 atm × V_2

V_2 = 4.00 L·atm ÷ 2.50 atm = **1.60 L**

Check: the pressure went up, so the volume should go down. It did.

## Common mistake

Multiplying where you should divide. Use the check above: if pressure rises, volume must fall. If your answer moved the wrong way, the ratio is upside down.
