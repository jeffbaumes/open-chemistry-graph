# Molarity

**Concentration** tells how much solute is in a given amount of solution. The most common unit in chemistry is **molarity** (M).

molarity = moles of solute ÷ liters of solution, or M = n / V

A 1.0 M solution contains 1.0 mol of solute in every liter of solution. M is read as "molar" and means mol/L.

## Worked example: find the molarity

0.50 mol of NaCl is dissolved to make 2.0 L of solution.

M = 0.50 mol ÷ 2.0 L = **0.25 M**

## Worked example: starting from grams

What is the molarity of 10.0 g of NaOH in 500 mL of solution?

Moles: 10.0 g ÷ 39.997 g/mol = 0.250 mol

Volume: 500 mL = 0.500 L

M = 0.250 mol ÷ 0.500 L = **0.500 M**

## Worked example: find the moles

How many moles of solute are in 0.250 L of a 2.00 M solution? Rearrange to n = M × V.

n = 2.00 mol/L × 0.250 L = **0.500 mol**

## Common mistake

Dividing by milliliters. Molarity uses liters. Also, the volume is that of the final solution, not the water you started with.
