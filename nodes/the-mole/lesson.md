# The mole

Atoms are far too small to count one at a time, so chemists count them in a very large group called the **mole** (mol).

1 mol = 6.022 × 10^23 particles

This number is **Avogadro's number**. A mole works like a dozen: a dozen eggs is 12 eggs, and a mole of atoms is 6.022 × 10^23 atoms. The particles can be atoms, molecules, ions or formula units.

- moles to particles: **multiply** by 6.022 × 10^23
- particles to moles: **divide** by 6.022 × 10^23

## Worked example: moles to particles

How many molecules are in 2.50 mol of water?

2.50 mol × 6.022 × 10^23 molecules/mol = **1.51 × 10^24 molecules**

## Worked example: particles to moles

How many moles is 3.01 × 10^23 atoms of copper?

3.01 × 10^23 atoms ÷ 6.022 × 10^23 atoms/mol = **0.500 mol**

## Common mistake

Forgetting that a mole of molecules contains more than a mole of atoms. One mole of H2O contains 2 mol of hydrogen atoms and 1 mol of oxygen atoms.
