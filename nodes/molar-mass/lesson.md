# Molar mass

The molar mass of a substance is the mass of one mole of it, in grams per mole (g/mol).

For an element, it is the atomic mass from the periodic table: one mole of carbon has a mass of 12.011 g.

For a compound, add up the atomic masses of every atom in the formula.

## Worked example: H2O

| Element | Atoms | Atomic mass | Subtotal |
|---|---|---|---|
| H | 2 | 1.008 | 2.016 |
| O | 1 | 15.999 | 15.999 |

Molar mass = 2.016 + 15.999 = **18.015 g/mol**

## Parentheses

A subscript after parentheses multiplies everything inside. Ca(OH)2 contains 1 Ca, 2 O and 2 H.

40.078 + 2(15.999) + 2(1.008) = **74.09 g/mol**

## Common mistake

Forgetting to distribute the outer subscript. In Al2(SO4)3 there are 3 × 4 = 12 oxygen atoms, not 4.
