# Ions

An **ion** is an atom that has gained or lost electrons, so it has a charge.

- Losing electrons leaves more protons than electrons: a positive ion, or **cation**.
- Gaining electrons gives more electrons than protons: a negative ion, or **anion**.

charge = protons - electrons

Atoms tend to gain or lose electrons to reach a full outer level. Metals have few valence electrons and lose them. Nonmetals have many and gain a few more.

| Group | Valence electrons | Typical ion |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1 | 1+ |
| 2 | 2 | 2+ |
| 13 | 3 | 3+ |
| 15 | 5 | 3- |
| 16 | 6 | 2- |
| 17 | 7 | 1- |

## Worked example: magnesium

Magnesium is in group 2, with 2 valence electrons. Losing both leaves 12 protons and 10 electrons.

12 - 10 = +2, so the ion is **Mg^2+**

## Common mistake

Thinking a positive ion gained something. A positive charge means electrons were lost. Protons never change when an ion forms.
