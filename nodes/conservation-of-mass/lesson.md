# Conservation of mass

In a chemical reaction, atoms are rearranged. They are not created or destroyed. So the total mass of the **reactants** (the starting substances) equals the total mass of the **products** (the substances formed).

This is the **law of conservation of mass**.

A **chemical equation** describes a reaction. Reactants are on the left, products are on the right, and the arrow means "produces".

**2 H2 + O2 -> 2 H2O**

## Worked example: burning magnesium

24.3 g of magnesium burns and forms 40.3 g of magnesium oxide. How much oxygen reacted?

mass of reactants = mass of products

24.3 g + mass of oxygen = 40.3 g

mass of oxygen = 40.3 g - 24.3 g = **16.0 g**

## Common mistake

Concluding that mass was lost when a reaction gives off a gas. A burning log leaves light ash because most of the products, carbon dioxide and water vapor, escape into the air. In a sealed container the total mass does not change.
