# Chemical equilibrium

Many reactions are **reversible**: the products can react to form the reactants again. A double arrow shows this.

**N2 + 3 H2 <-> 2 NH3**

At first the forward reaction is fast. As products build up, the reverse reaction speeds up. **Equilibrium** is reached when the forward and reverse rates are equal. From then on the amounts stay constant, although both reactions continue.

**Le Chatelier's principle:** if a system at equilibrium is disturbed, it shifts in the direction that partly undoes the change.

| Change | Shift |
|---|---|
| Add a reactant | Toward products |
| Remove a product | Toward products |
| Add a product | Toward reactants |
| Raise the temperature | In the endothermic direction |
| Raise the pressure (gases) | Toward the side with fewer moles of gas |

A catalyst does not shift equilibrium. It only helps the system reach it faster.

## Worked example: the pressure is raised on N2 + 3 H2 <-> 2 NH3

The left side has 1 + 3 = 4 mol of gas and the right side has 2 mol. Raising the pressure shifts the reaction **toward NH3**, the side with fewer moles of gas.

## Common mistake

Thinking the reaction stops at equilibrium, or that the amounts of reactants and products are equal. The rates are equal. The amounts are constant but usually not equal.
