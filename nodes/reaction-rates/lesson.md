# Reaction rates

The **rate** of a reaction is how fast reactants turn into products.

**Collision theory** says particles react only when they collide with enough energy and in the right orientation. The minimum energy needed is the **activation energy**, E_a. Most collisions fall short of it and the particles just bounce apart.

Anything that causes more collisions, or more energetic ones, speeds up a reaction.

| Factor | Effect | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Higher temperature | Faster | Particles move faster, so more collisions exceed E_a |
| Higher concentration | Faster | More particles in the same space collide more often |
| More surface area | Faster | More of a solid is exposed to collisions |
| Catalyst | Faster | Provides a path with a lower E_a |

A **catalyst** speeds up a reaction without being used up. It does not change ΔH or the amount of product. Enzymes are the catalysts of living things.

## Worked example: why does powdered zinc react faster than a lump of zinc?

The powder has far more surface area. More zinc atoms are exposed at once, so there are more collisions per second with the other reactant.

## Common mistake

Thinking a catalyst adds energy or makes more product. It only lowers the energy barrier, so the same products form sooner.
