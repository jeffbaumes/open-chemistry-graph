# Mole ratios in reactions

The coefficients in a balanced equation give the ratio in which substances react, counted in moles.

**N2 + 3 H2 -> 2 NH3**

This says 1 mol of N2 reacts with 3 mol of H2 to make 2 mol of NH3. Any two substances in the equation give a **mole ratio**, which works as a conversion factor.

Using these ratios to calculate amounts is called **stoichiometry**.

## Worked example: how many moles of NH3 form from 6.0 mol of H2?

The equation relates 3 mol of H2 to 2 mol of NH3.

6.0 mol H2 × (2 mol NH3 / 3 mol H2) = **4.0 mol NH3**

The unit mol H2 cancels, leaving mol NH3.

## Worked example: how many moles of N2 react with 6.0 mol of H2?

6.0 mol H2 × (1 mol N2 / 3 mol H2) = **2.0 mol N2**

## Common mistake

Using an unbalanced equation, or using the ratio upside down. Always balance first, then put the substance you want on top of the ratio.
