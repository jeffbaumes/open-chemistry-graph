# Acids and bases

- An **acid** produces hydrogen ions, H^+, in water. Acids taste sour and react with many metals.
- A **base** produces hydroxide ions, OH^-, in water. Bases taste bitter and feel slippery.

HCl -> H^+ + Cl^-

NaOH -> Na^+ + OH^-

A broader definition covers more cases: an acid is a **proton donor** and a base is a **proton acceptor**. A hydrogen ion is just a proton. Ammonia, NH3, has no OH in its formula but is a base because it accepts H^+ from water.

In water, H^+ attaches to a water molecule to form the hydronium ion, H3O^+. The two symbols are used interchangeably.

- A **strong** acid or base breaks apart completely into ions. HCl, HNO3 and H2SO4 are strong acids. NaOH and KOH are strong bases.
- A **weak** acid or base ionizes only slightly. Acetic acid (in vinegar) and ammonia are weak.

## Worked example: classify HNO3 and Ca(OH)2

HNO3 releases H^+ in water, so it is an acid. Ca(OH)2 releases OH^-, so it is a base.

## Common mistake

Confusing strong with concentrated. Strength is about how completely the substance ionizes. Concentration is about how much is dissolved. A dilute solution of a strong acid and a concentrated solution of a weak acid are both possible.
