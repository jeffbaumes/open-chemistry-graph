# The pH scale

The **pH** of a solution measures how acidic it is. It is based on the molar concentration of hydrogen ions, written [H^+].

**pH = -log [H^+]**

| pH | Solution |
|---|---|
| Below 7 | Acidic |
| 7 | Neutral |
| Above 7 | Basic |

The scale is logarithmic. Each step of 1 pH unit is a factor of 10 in [H^+]. A solution at pH 3 has ten times the hydrogen ion concentration of one at pH 4.

Water itself ionizes very slightly, which links the two ions. At 25 °C:

[H^+] × [OH^-] = 1.0 × 10^-14

In pure water both are 1.0 × 10^-7 M, so the pH is 7. A matching scale for hydroxide gives pH + pOH = 14.

## Worked example: find the pH

A solution has [H^+] = 1.0 × 10^-3 M.

pH = -log(1.0 × 10^-3) = **3.00**

When [H^+] is exactly a power of ten, the pH is that exponent with the sign changed.

## Worked example: find the hydrogen ion concentration

A solution has pH 9. Reverse the logarithm:

[H^+] = 10^-9 M

The pH is above 7, so the solution is **basic**.

## Common mistake

Thinking a higher pH means more acidic. It is the reverse: the lower the pH, the higher [H^+].
