# Chemical formulas

A **chemical formula** uses element symbols and subscripts to show what a substance is made of.

- A **subscript** after a symbol tells how many of that atom there are. No subscript means 1.
- **Parentheses** group atoms together. A subscript after the parentheses multiplies everything inside.
- A **coefficient** in front of the formula tells how many whole units there are. It multiplies everything in the formula.

For a molecular substance, the formula describes one molecule. For an ionic compound, it gives the simplest ratio of ions.

## Worked example: Ca(NO3)2

| Element | Count |
|---|---|
| Ca | 1 |
| N | 1 × 2 = 2 |
| O | 3 × 2 = 6 |

One formula unit contains 9 atoms.

## Worked example: 3 H2O

Each water molecule has 2 hydrogen atoms and 1 oxygen atom. Three molecules have 6 hydrogen atoms and 3 oxygen atoms.

## Common mistake

Reading a two-letter symbol as two elements. Co is cobalt, one element. CO is carbon monoxide, a compound of carbon and oxygen. The second letter of a symbol is always lowercase.
