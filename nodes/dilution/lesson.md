# Dilution

**Diluting** a solution means adding solvent. The amount of solute stays the same, but it is spread through a larger volume, so the concentration drops.

Because the moles of solute do not change, and moles = M × V:

M_1 × V_1 = M_2 × V_2

The 1 values describe the concentrated starting solution and the 2 values describe the diluted one. The volumes can be in any unit as long as both use the same one.

## Worked example: diluting an acid

What volume of 12.0 M HCl is needed to make 500 mL of 3.00 M HCl?

M_1 × V_1 = M_2 × V_2

12.0 M × V_1 = 3.00 M × 500 mL

V_1 = 1500 M·mL ÷ 12.0 M = **125 mL**

Measure out 125 mL of the concentrated acid and add water until the total volume is 500 mL.

## Common mistake

Reading V_2 as the volume of water added. V_2 is the final total volume. In the example, the water added is 500 - 125 = 375 mL.
