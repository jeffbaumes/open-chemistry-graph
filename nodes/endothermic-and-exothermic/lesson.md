# Endothermic and exothermic reactions

Breaking bonds takes energy. Forming bonds releases energy. A reaction's overall heat change depends on which is larger.

- An **exothermic** reaction releases heat to its surroundings, which get warmer. Burning fuel is exothermic.
- An **endothermic** reaction absorbs heat from its surroundings, which get cooler. An instant cold pack is endothermic.

The heat change at constant pressure is the **enthalpy change**, ΔH.

| Reaction | Heat | Sign of ΔH | Products compared with reactants |
|---|---|---|---|
| Exothermic | Released | Negative | Lower in energy |
| Endothermic | Absorbed | Positive | Higher in energy |

ΔH is written for the equation as balanced:

CH4 + 2 O2 -> CO2 + 2 H2O, with ΔH = -890 kJ

Burning 1 mol of CH4 releases 890 kJ.

## Worked example: how much heat is released by burning 3.00 mol of CH4?

3.00 mol × 890 kJ/mol = **2670 kJ released**

## Common mistake

Thinking an exothermic reaction feels cold because it "loses" heat. The reaction gives its heat to you and the surroundings, so it feels hot.
